\subsection{Structural models}

The global-minimum structures of \ce{Pt10} and \ce{Pt5Ge5} reported by
Ugartemendia \textit{et al.}\cite{ugartemendia:2022} were used to generate
two sets of derivatives by Pt/Ge substitution, summarized in
Figure~\ref{fig:crossed_models}. Here, the superscript $GM$ denotes a reported
global-minimum structure reoptimized in this work, and $der$ denotes a structure
generated by Pt/Ge substitution. In the \ce{Pt10}-to-Pt--Ge derivation,
selected Pt atoms in \ce{Pt10^{GM}} were replaced by Ge
\green{to give an approximately alternating Pt/Ge arrangement, similar to
that in \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}}.} This produced
\ce{Pt6Ge4^{der}}, \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6^{der}}. In the reverse
\ce{Pt5Ge5}-to-\ce{Pt10} derivation, all Ge atoms in
\ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} were replaced by Pt to form \ce{Pt10^{der}}. All these
structures were optimized at the level of theory described above.

\subsection{Structure, spin state, and local Pt environments}


With the structural models defined above, we now describe their geometries, spin
states, and local Pt environments.
%Figure~\ref{fig:crossed_models} shows the two reported GM structures, \(\ce{Pt10^{GM}}\) and \(\ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}}\).

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:crossed_models}\textcolor{purple}{,} the \ce{Pt10^{GM}} cluster has a compact, highly symmetric pyramidal geometry and a
nonet ground state. The \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} cluster also forms a compact cage.
Four \ce{Pt} atoms form a roughly square group around one Ge atom. On the opposite
side of the cage, four Ge atoms surround one Pt atom in a similar arrangement.
This structure has a singlet ground state.

For the two \ce{Pt10} isomers, \ce{Pt10^{GM}} is a nonet, whereas
\ce{Pt10^{der}} is a triplet and lies 1.29 eV higher in $E_0$. For the
adsorption study, we also considered the quintet state of
\ce{Pt10^{der}}, which lies 0.04 eV above its triplet and allows us to
examine the effect of spin state.

The optimized \ce{Pt6Ge4^{der}}, \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}}, and
\ce{Pt4Ge6^{der}} structures remain compact, but have lower symmetry than
\ce{Pt10^{GM}} and distinct local Pt/Ge environments. All three derivatives
are singlets, 0.18--0.26 eV below their corresponding triplet states. The
\ce{Pt6Ge4} GM is a triplet, while the \ce{Pt5Ge5} and \ce{Pt4Ge6} GMs are
singlets\cite{ugartemendia:2022}. The singlet preference is therefore clearer for the Pt--Ge derivatives
than for \ce{Pt10^{der}}.

The energy differences between the derivatives and their corresponding
GMs vary with composition. For \ce{Pt6Ge4}, the singlet derivative lies
0.41 eV above the triplet GM. For \ce{Pt5Ge5}, the singlet derivative lies
1.73 eV above the singlet GM. The \ce{Pt4Ge6^{der}} and
\ce{Pt4Ge6^{GM}} singlets are effectively isoenergetic at this level of
theory.

The structures considered here provide Pt sites with different local
environments. We characterize these environments using ECoN and
$x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$. ECoN describes the total effective coordination, while
$x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$ gives the fraction of this coordination provided by Ge
neighbors before \ce{CO} adsorption. Together, these descriptors describe
the extent and the Pt/Ge composition of the local coordination environment.

For \ce{Pt10^{GM}}, six Pt atoms have initial $ECoN^{0}$ values close to
5.68, while the other four have values of 3.00. In \ce{Pt10^{der}}, the
initial values range from
3.03 to \green{5.94} and form five pairs of similar sites (Supporting
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_pt10_bare_econ}). A higher initial $ECoN^{0}$ indicates a more highly
coordinated local environment; a lower value indicates lower effective
coordination. The Pt--Ge structures also differ in their total coordination
and in the relative contributions of Pt and Ge neighbors. Their values are
given in
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_crossed_ptge_econ_descriptors} and Supporting
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_gm_ptge_econ_pre_descriptors}.

This range of local Pt environments allows us to examine how coordination
and local Pt/Ge composition relate to \ce{CO} adsorption.
