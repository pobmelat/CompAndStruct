\subsection{Starting-structure effects in \ce{Pt10} and Pt--Ge clusters}

Figure~\ref{fig:crossed_models} compares the two reported GM starting
structures. The all-Pt \ce{Pt10^{GM}} structure is a compact, highly symmetric
cluster with a nonet ground state. The equimolar \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} structure is a compact
singlet structure with distinct Pt and Ge positions. The two starting
structures therefore differ in both geometry and multiplicity before any atom
substitution is made.

Replacing selected Pt atoms in \ce{Pt10^{GM}} with Ge gives the
\ce{Pt6Ge4^{der}}, \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6^{der}} models in the lower left part of
Figure~\ref{fig:crossed_models}. They retain compact ten-atom structures, but
substitution and subsequent relaxation lower the symmetry and create Pt sites
with different Pt/Ge environments. In the reverse derivation, replacing all Ge
atoms in \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} with Pt gives \ce{Pt10^{der}}, a lower-symmetry triplet structure.

For the derivative adsorption series, this triplet
\ce{Pt10^{der}} structure is used as the pure-Pt model; its
nearby quintet is included to test the effect of spin state. The
\ce{Pt6Ge4^{der}}, \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6^{der}} structures derived from \ce{Pt10^{GM}}
are used as singlets. The separately optimized GM structures provide a second
set of adsorption calculations: \ce{Pt10^{GM}} is a nonet, \ce{Pt6Ge4^{GM}}
is a triplet, and \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} and \ce{Pt4Ge6^{GM}} are singlets.

At the PBEPBE/def2-TZVP level, \ce{Pt10^{der}} is
lowest as a triplet, and the quintet is 0.04 eV
higher in $\Delta E_0$. The selected triplet wavefunction and the seven
distinct triplet \ce{Pt10CO} minima were confirmed to be orbital-stable.
The \ce{Pt10}-to-\ce{Pt-Ge} models favor
the closed-shell singlet over the triplet by 0.18--0.26 eV. Thus, within this
set of models, the Ge-containing clusters have a clearer preference for
the low-spin state than \ce{Pt10^{der}}.

The \ce{Pt10^{GM}} and \ce{Pt10^{der}} structures have the same composition
but remain distinct after optimization. \ce{Pt10^{GM}} is a nonet, whereas
\ce{Pt10^{der}} is a triplet. At the
present level, the latter lies 1.29 eV above the reported GM in $E_0$. This
difference combines the effects of their distinct structures and multiplicities.

Their local Pt environments also differ. The ECoN values for \ce{Pt10^{GM}}
fall into two groups: six Pt atoms have values close to 5.68, whereas four have
an ECoN of 3.00. In the \ce{Pt10^{der}} triplet, the ECoN values span
3.03--6.01 and define five pairs of similar Pt environments. All ECoN values
for the individual Pt atoms are given in Supporting
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_pt10_bare_econ}.

We use the same comparison for the Pt--Ge compositions,
summarizing the descriptors across the sampled Pt sites so that the GM and
derivative structures can be compared by their overall local
environments. For \ce{Pt6Ge4}, the \ce{Pt6Ge4^{GM}} values span a
narrower range of total ECoN (3.84--4.87) than the \ce{Pt10}-to-\ce{Pt-Ge} models
(2.77--4.60), while both structures include Pt sites with a broad range of Ge
character. The difference is clearer for \ce{Pt5Ge5}: the \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} Pt sites are
uniformly Ge-rich ($x_{\rm Ge}^{0}=0.930$--0.998), whereas the
\ce{Pt10}-to-\ce{Pt-Ge} models include Pt sites with lower Ge character
($x_{\rm Ge}^{0}=0.745$) and a wider ECoN range (2.91--5.58 versus
3.48--4.03). The \ce{Pt4Ge6^{der}} and \ce{Pt4Ge6^{GM}} structures are Ge-rich in both cases, but still
sample different distributions of total coordination. The detailed descriptors
for the derivative structures and for the GM structures are given in
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_crossed_ptge_econ_descriptors} and Supporting
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_gm_ptge_econ_pre_descriptors}, respectively. These
comparisons show that changing the starting structure changes the local Pt
environments available for \ce{CO} adsorption, even at fixed composition.
The next section examines how these structural differences are reflected in
the bonding patterns.
