\subsection{Structural models}

The global-minimum structures of \ce{Pt10} and \ce{Pt5Ge5} reported by Ugartemendia \textit{et al.}\cite{ugartemendia:2022} were used to generate two sets of derivatives by Pt/Ge substitution, summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:crossed_models}. Here, the superscript $GM$ denotes a reported global-minimum structure reoptimized in this work, and $der$ denotes a structure generated by Pt/Ge substitution. In the \ce{Pt10}-to-Pt--Ge derivation,
selected Pt atoms in \ce{Pt10^{GM}} were replaced by Ge to give an approximately alternating Pt/Ge arrangement, similar to that in \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}}. This produced \ce{Pt6Ge4^{der}}, \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6^{der}}. In the reverse \ce{Pt5Ge5}-to-\ce{Pt10} derivation, all Ge atoms in \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} were replaced by Pt to form \ce{Pt10^{der}}. All these
structures were optimized at the level of theory described above.

\subsection{Structure, spin state, and local Pt environments}


%Figure~\ref{fig:crossed_models} shows the two reported GM structures, \(\ce{Pt10^{GM}}\) and \(\ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}}\).

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:crossed_models}\textcolor{purple}{,} the \ce{Pt10^{GM}} cluster has a compact tetrahedral structure with four Pt atoms occupying the vertices and six additional Pt atoms located along the edges. Its ground spin-state is a
nonet. The \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} cluster also forms a compact cage. Its structure can be described as a double square-pyramidal geometry, which exhibits exclusively heteroatomic Pt–Ge bonds. This structure has a singlet ground spin state.

In previous works\cite{ugartemendia:2021,ugartemendia:2024,poths:2023b} it was demonstrated that clusters with roughly the same amount of Pt and Ge are less reactive, namely, form weaker bonds with adsorbates such as CO as compared to monometallic Pt clusters. As a consequence, these compounds can be more selective or even become catalytically inactive. With the newly derived isomers, we aim to establish if the effect of Ge tempering the reactivity has its roots in the Pt-Ge chemical bond or if the geometry plays a fundamental role. Starting from \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}}  the  \ce{Pt10^{der}} structure is obtained with a triplet ground spin state and lies 1.29 eV higher. Noteworthy, the \ce{Pt10^{der}} structure in a quintet state of is almost degenerated and lies 0.04 eV above its triplet.

Regarding the bimetallic compounds, the optimized \ce{Pt6Ge4^{der}}, \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6^{der}} structures remain compact, but have lower symmetry than \ce{Pt10^{GM}}. While the \ce{Pt6Ge4} GM is a triplet, the \ce{Pt5Ge5} and \ce{Pt4Ge6} GMs are singlets\cite{ugartemendia:2022},  all three PtGe derivatives are singlets, with their corresponding triplet states lying above 0.18--0.26 eV. The singlet preference is therefore clearer for the Pt--Ge derivatives than for \ce{Pt10^{der}}.

The energy differences between the derivatives and their corresponding GMs depend strongly on composition. For \ce{Pt6Ge4}, the singlet derivative lies 0.41 eV above the triplet GM, whereas for \ce{Pt5Ge5}, the singlet derivative is 1.73 eV higher in energy than the singlet GM. In contrast,  the \ce{Pt4Ge6^{der}} and \ce{Pt4Ge6^{GM}} singlets are effectively isoenergetic at this level of theory.

The structures considered here provide Pt sites with different local environments. We characterize these environments using ECoN and $x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$. ECoN describes the total effective coordination, while $x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$ gives the fraction of this coordination provided by Ge neighbors. Together, these descriptors identify the extent and the Pt/Ge composition of the local coordination environment.

For \ce{Pt10^{GM}}, six Pt atoms have initial $ECoN^{0}$ values close to 5.68, while the other four have values of 3.00. In \ce{Pt10^{der}}, the initial values range from 3.03 to \green{5.94} and form five pairs of similar sites (Supporting Table~\ref{tab:tfg_pt10_bare_econ}). A higher initial $ECoN^{0}$ indicates a more 
coordinated local environment; a lower value indicates a lower effective coordination. The Pt--Ge structures also differ in their total coordination
and in the relative contributions of Pt and Ge neighbors. Their values are
given in Table~\ref{tab:tfg_crossed_ptge_econ_descriptors} and Supporting
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_gm_ptge_econ_pre_descriptors}.

This range of local Pt environments allows us to examine how coordination
and local Pt/Ge composition influence the \ce{CO} adsorption.
