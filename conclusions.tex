Carbon monoxide poisoning is a major deactivation mechanism for Pt in various catalytic applications leading to large economic expenses associated with the renewal of the catalysts in industry. Previous works showed that Ge doping can increase the resistance to CO poisoning. However, Ge incorporation into Pt also changes the geometry and bonding pattern of Pt clusters and it is unclear how Ge weakens the \ce{Pt-CO} interaction. In this work, using model 10-atom subnanoclusters, we determine the interplay between chemical bonding, geometry, spin state and tendency to undergo CO poisoning. The work is based on a crossed-parent framework, in which the geometry of ideal pure Pt and equimolar PtGe clusters is preserved. The derivatives are subsequently complexed to CO and their electronic structure, chemical bonding and affinities to CO are studied.

We used Pt/Ge substitution in ten-atom clusters to examine how composition
and cluster structure affect \ce{CO} binding. Within the Pt--Ge derivative series, the mean $BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ becomes less negative as the Ge content increases, although the binding energies vary between Pt sites. AdNDP describes delocalized multicenter bonding in \ce{Pt10^{der}}, even though this isomer was generated from the \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} structure. The Pt--Ge derivatives instead show mainly localized one- and two-center bonding.

The comparison of the two \ce{Pt10} quintet series shows the effect of
structure at fixed composition and multiplicity. Their mean binding energies
differ by about 0.30 eV, with stronger mean binding for \ce{Pt10^{der}}.
The Pt--Ge GM and derivative structures also give different \ce{CO} binding
energies. For \ce{Pt6Ge4}, this comparison includes a change in multiplicity.

The initial ECoN descriptors distinguish the coordination and local Pt/Ge composition of 
the adsorption sites. Sites with a larger initial Ge fraction tend to bind \(\ce{CO}\) less strongly.
The comparison between sites 6/8 and 7/10 of \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}CO} illustrates
why local descriptors need to be considered together with cluster
reorganization. These sites have almost identical initial Ge fractions and
similar optimized \ce{Pt-C} distances and BCP densities, but their binding
energies differ by 0.42 eV. The fragment decomposition relates this difference
mainly to metal-cluster reorganization.

Changes in ECoN describe local rearrangement, while the reorganization energies measure its energy cost or stabilization. 
Across the selected sites and compositions, differences in
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ reflect different balances between cluster--CO
interaction and metal-cluster reorganization, relative to the selected
bare-cluster reference. Combining binding energies with local descriptors
and fragment energies helps identify the contributions of bonding and
structural relaxation to CO adsorption.

We argue that the binding energy to CO, being a global descriptor that depends not only on the energy of the cluster--CO complex but also on the energy of the reference states chosen for the bare cluster, is not necessarily the best descriptor for measuring CO affinity.
