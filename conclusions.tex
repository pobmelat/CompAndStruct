
We used Pt/Ge substitution in ten-atom clusters to examine how composition
and cluster structure affect \ce{CO} binding. Within the
Pt--Ge derivative series, the mean
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ becomes less negative as the Ge content increases,
although the binding energies vary between Pt sites. AdNDP describes
delocalized multicenter bonding in \ce{Pt10^{der}}, even though this isomer
was generated from the \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} structure. The Pt--Ge derivatives
instead show mainly localized one- and two-center bonding.

The comparison of the two \ce{Pt10} quintet series shows the effect of
structure at fixed composition and multiplicity. Their mean binding energies
differ by about 0.30 eV, with stronger mean binding for \ce{Pt10^{der}}.
{The GM-based Pt--Ge calculations also show that different
isomers of the same composition can give different \ce{CO} binding energies.}

The initial ECoN descriptors distinguish the coordination and local Pt/Ge composition of 
the adsorption sites. Sites with a larger initial Ge fraction tend to bind \(\ce{CO}\) less strongly.
Sites 6/8 and 7/10 of \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}CO} have almost
identical initial Ge fractions and similar optimized \ce{Pt-C} distances
and BCP densities, but their binding energies differ by 0.42 eV.
The fragment decomposition shows that this difference is mainly
associated with metal-cluster reorganization.

Changes in ECoN describe local rearrangement, while the reorganization energies measure its energy cost or stabilization. 
The fragment analysis shows that the relative contributions of interaction and metal reorganization vary between sites and compositions.
Thus, \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) reflects their combined energetic effects,
relative to the selected bare-cluster reference. Local descriptors and fragment energies help us 
understand the origin of these binding-energy differences.
