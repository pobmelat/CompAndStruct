\subsection{Bonding patterns in Pt and Pt--Ge clusters}

The AdNDP analysis was used to analyze the bonding patterns of the Pt and Pt--Ge
clusters. AdNDP provides a unified framework for describing both localized and delocalized chemical bonding through \textit{n}--center two-electron (nc--2e) and \textit{n}--center one-electron (nc--1e) bonds. Monometallic and bimetallic clusters show a very different bonding pattern \cite{ugartemendia:2022}. On the one hand, \ce{Pt10^{GM}} exhibits a fully delocalized bonding with 30 lone-pairs (ON = $1.83|e|$), the rest of the electrons are delocalized over 16 3c--2e bonds (ON = $1.97|e|$) on each external triangular face of the cluster, 4 3c--1e bonds (ON = $0.99|e|$) on the internal triangular faces and 4 4c–1e bonds (ON = $0.93|e|$). On the other hand, all the electron pairs in \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} can be localized in lone-pairs 15 1c--2e bonds (ON = $1.84|e|$) (three on each Pt) and 20 covalent 2c–2e bonds (ON = $1.86|e|$) between every Pt--Ge pair. Therefore, the delocalized metallic bonding dominates in pure Pt clusters, but Pt and Ge bond through strong covalent directional bonds. Now, does this bonding pattern remain even if the geometries are exchanged? 
For triplet \ce{Pt10^{der}}, the $\alpha$ and $\beta$ density matrices were analyzed separately. AdNDP localizes the electrons in three Pt-centered 1c--2e elements per Pt atom, 19 3c--2e bonds, and two additional 3c--1e bonds  (Supporting Table~\ref{tab:tfg_adndp_summary}), therefore describing a delocalized multicenter bonding. By contrast, \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}} exhibits a localized bonding, with 20 Pt-centered 1c--2e elements, 14 Pt--Ge 2c--2e bonds, and three Pt--Pt 2c--2e bonds (Supporting Table~\ref{tab:tfg_adndp_summary}). The \ce{Pt6Ge4} and \ce{Pt4Ge6} derivatives also show one- and two-center elements. Taken together, these results suggest that the preference for a metallic bonding in Pt and a directional covalent bond in PtGe dominates these structures beyond a particular geometry.
Figure~\ref{fig:tfg_adndp_comparison} shows selected examples: one multicenter element from \ce{Pt10^{der}} and two localized bonds from \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}}. The AdNDP analysis for all derivative isomers is summarized in Supporting Table~\ref{tab:tfg_adndp_summary}.
