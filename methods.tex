All cluster calculations were performed with Gaussian~16\cite{g16}
using the PBE exchange--correlation functional.\cite{perdew:pbe:1996}
A def2-TZVP basis set was used for C, O, and Ge. For Pt, the def2-TZVP
basis was used together with the corresponding Karlsruhe 60-electron
scalar-relativistic effective core potential (ECP).\cite{weigend:def2:2005,andrae:epp:1990}
The bare clusters, carbonyl complexes, and free \ce{CO} were optimized, and
their harmonic frequencies were calculated. All optimized structures
were confirmed as minima by the absence of imaginary frequencies.
Orbital stability was tested for the selected triplet \ce{Pt10} structure
and for the seven distinct triplet \ce{Pt10CO} minima obtained from the
independent Pt-site placements. All tested wavefunctions were stable under
the perturbations considered.

The reported relative energies and \ce{CO} binding
energies include the harmonic zero-point vibrational energy (ZPVE).
Hereafter, ``carbonyl'' denotes the optimized cluster--\ce{CO} complex. We define
\begin{align}
E_0(X) &= E_{\rm elec}(X)+\mathrm{ZPVE}(X),\\
BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO}) &= E_0(\mathrm{carbonyl})
              -E_0(\mathrm{bare\ cluster})-E_0(\ce{CO}).
\label{eq:neutral_dft_be}
\end{align}
Thus, more negative values correspond to stronger adsorption.
Unless stated otherwise, each state-specific adiabatic \ce{CO} binding energy, \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\), is calculated relative to free singlet \ce{CO} and the bare-cluster reference selected for the same structural model and multiplicity as the carbonyl. The resulting \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) includes both the cluster--\ce{CO} interaction and the energy change associated with structural relaxation.
For simplicity, hereafter, ``binding energy'' and \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) refer
to this state-specific adiabatic \ce{CO} binding energy.

Hoppe’s effective coordination number (ECoN) was used to characterize the local environment of each Pt adsorption site.\cite{hoppe:econ:1979} ECoN descriptors were evaluated for the bare clusters before \ce{CO} adsorption and, where indicated, for the optimized carbonyls. The full definition of ECoN, together with the parameters and Pt/Ge partitioning used here, is given in the Supporting Information, Section ``Effective coordination number.''

We analyzed the bonding patterns with AdNDP~2.0 (adaptive natural density
partitioning).\cite{zubarev:adndp:2008,tkachenko:adndp2:2019}
%AdNDP analyses used the chemically active valence space, with ten electrons per Pt atom and four per Ge atom.

For the selected carbonyl minima included in the interaction and
fragment-reorganization analysis, the electron density was examined within the
atoms-in-molecules (AIM) framework using AIMPAC.\cite{aimpac,biegler:extreme:1981,biegler:proaim:1982}
The electron density at the Pt--C bond critical point, $\rho_{\rm BCP}$, was
obtained from the wavefunction of each optimized carbonyl.

For selected strongly and weakly bound carbonyl minima of \ce{Pt10},
\ce{Pt6Ge4}, \ce{Pt5Ge5}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6}, the electronic binding energy
was decomposed into frozen-fragment interaction and fragment-reorganization
terms, as described in Section~\ref{subsec:interaction_fragment_reorganization}.
