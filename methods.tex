All cluster calculations were performed with Gaussian~16\cite{g16}
using the PBE exchange--correlation functional (PBEPBE).\cite{perdew:pbe:1996}
A def2-TZVP basis was used for C, O, and Ge,\cite{weigend:def2:2005}
and Pt was described with the corresponding Karlsruhe valence basis and a
60-electron {scalar relativistic effective core potential (ECP).\cite{andrae:epp:1990,weigend:def2:2005}
This leaves 18 explicit Pt electrons.
We optimized the bare clusters, carbonyl complexes, and free \ce{CO}, and calculated their harmonic frequencies. All structures had no imaginary frequencies. Orbital stability was also tested for the selected triplet \ce{Pt10} structure and for the seven distinct triplet \ce{Pt10CO} minima obtained from the independent Pt-site placements.
All these wavefunctions were stable under the
perturbations considered.

The reported GM structure of each composition was also reoptimized at the
same level and used as a separate comparison set.

For this DFT series, the reported relative energies and \ce{CO} binding
energies include the harmonic zero-point vibrational energy (ZPVE).
Hereafter, ``carbonyl'' denotes the optimized cluster--\ce{CO} complex. We define
\begin{align}
E_0(X) &= E_{\rm elec}(X)+\mathrm{ZPVE}(X),\\
BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO}) &= E_0(\mathrm{carbonyl})
              -E_0(\mathrm{bare\ cluster})-E_0(\ce{CO}).
\label{eq:neutral_dft_be}
\end{align}
Thus, more negative values correspond to stronger adsorption.
Each value is a state-specific adiabatic \ce{CO} binding energy, \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\), calculated relative to free singlet \ce{CO} and the corresponding bare-cluster minimum. This reference is obtained by removing \ce{CO} from the optimized carbonyl and reoptimizing the remaining cluster at the same multiplicity.
This procedure defines a local reference for each carbonyl. The resulting \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) combines the direct cluster--\ce{CO} interaction with the energy change caused by structural relaxation.
For simplicity, hereafter, ``binding energy'' and \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) refer
to this state-specific adiabatic \ce{CO} binding energy.

Hoppe’s effective coordination number (ECoN) was used to characterize the local environment of each Pt adsorption site.\cite{hoppe:econ:1979} ECoN descriptors were evaluated for the bare clusters before \ce{CO} adsorption and, where indicated, for the optimized carbonyls. The full definition of ECoN, together with the parameters and Pt/Ge partitioning used here, is given in the Supporting Information, Section ``Effective coordination number.''

The bonding patterns were analyzed with the adaptive natural density partitioning tool (AdNDP~2.0).\cite{tkachenko:adndp2:2019}
AdNDP analyses used the chemically active valence space, with ten electrons per Pt atom and four per Ge atom.

For selected strongly and weakly bound carbonyl minima of \ce{Pt10}, \ce{Pt6Ge4}, \ce{Pt5Ge5}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6}, the electronic binding energy was decomposed into frozen-fragment interaction and fragment-reorganization terms. Single-point energies were evaluated for the bare cluster and \ce{CO} both at their isolated optimized geometries and at the geometries adopted in the carbonyl complex. The ZPVE correction was added afterward to obtain the final \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\).
