Platinum-based materials are particularly relevant in this context because of
their high activity in a wide range of
catalytic reactions such as in fuel-cell-related processes.\cite{jimenezizal:2018,jimenezizal:2019b,yu:2002,
zhang:2014,auer:1998} The performance, however, comes with a well-known
limitation, since Pt binds carbon monoxide strongly. Even small amounts of \ce{CO}
can block Pt active sites and cause severe deactivation, especially in
fuel-cell environments.\cite{cheng:2007,vogel:1975,baschuk:2001,
christoffersen:2001,yang:2021} Alloying Pt with a second element is 
a common strategy to tune the electronic structure of the active sites, reduce
Pt loading, and mitigate \ce{CO} poisoning.\cite{yousaf:2016,yin:2011,
stolbov:2009,lee:1999,hayden:2003,samjeske:2002,ferrari:2016,ahmad:2015,
ehteshami:2013,chen:2019,ribeiro:2019}

Germanium can modify the structure and adsorption properties of Pt-based
catalysts. Pt--Ge materials have shown changes in activity and stability in
electrocatalysis, CO oxidation, and hydrocarbon conversion.\cite{lim:2018,
veizaga:2014,veizaga:2019,crabb:2001,gyorffy:2009,poths:2023,vilella:2008,
rimaz:2019,jimenezizal:2019,ma:2023,garetto:1996,mazzieri:2009,mariscal:2007}
Experimental and theoretical studies on \ce{PtGe/C} electrocatalysts have also
shown modified \ce{CO} oxidation behavior relative to monometallic Pt
systems.\cite{veizaga:2014,veizaga:2019,crabb:2001} These observations suggest
that Ge can act through both geometric effects, by disrupting contiguous Pt
ensembles, and electronic effects, by changing the electron density and orbital
availability at Pt sites.
Experiments on \ce{Pt(111)-Ge} surface alloys also reported weaker \ce{CO}
chemisorption than on \ce{Pt(111)} and associated this change with the modified
electronic structure of Pt after Ge incorporation.\cite{fukutani:1996,fukutani:1998}
Earlier calculations on Pt--Ge cluster models likewise found weaker Pt--C
interactions and changes in the \ce{C-O} stretching frequency after Ge
substitution.\cite{liao:2000}

Gas-phase and supported clusters provide a controlled route to examine these
effects in atomically defined environments. A recent review of \ce{CO}
adsorption on doped metal clusters emphasizes the coupled roles of composition,
charge state, and local environment.\cite{ferrari:2020} For 13-atom Pt--Au
clusters, Morrow and co-workers showed that the local environment of the
adsorption site affects both the \ce{CO} binding energy and stretching
frequency.\cite{morrow:2011} Ugartemendia and co-workers combined density
functional theory (DFT)
with mass spectrometry to study \ce{Pt_n^+} and \ce{GePt_{n-1}^+} clusters
with $n=5$--9.\cite{ugartemendia:2021} Their global-minimum searches and
electronic-structure analyses showed that replacing one Pt atom by Ge generally
weakens the Pt--CO interaction while the clusters remain active toward
\ce{H2} dissociation. Within a Blyholder-type interpretation, the weaker
{\ce{CO} binding was assigned mainly to a reduced Pt $\rightarrow$ \ce{CO}
$\pi$ back-donation. In this interpretation, Pt--Ge orbital mixing competes
with the Pt orbitals that would otherwise interact with the \ce{CO} $2\pi^*$
acceptor manifold.}

A subsequent study extended this idea to
PtGe nanoclusters of different sizes and
compositions.\cite{ugartemendia:2022} and revealed a significantly strong resistance to undergo CO poisoning in clusters with roughly
equimolar Pt:Ge ratios. The authors traced down this behavior to the strong Pt--Ge
covalency and electrostatic stabilization from favorable Pt--Ge mixing.

In addition, the composition in PtGe is shown to influence the catalytic activity in surface-mounted nanoclusters, in reactions such as alkane dehydrogenation \cite{poths:2023b, li_size:2025} or CO oxidation \cite{ugartemendia:2024}. In the latter, the preferential binding of CO at Pt and oxygen at Ge sites lead to a bifunctional mechanism, while weaker CO adsorption reduced self-poisoning.

These studies show that Ge changes both Pt--Ge bonding and CO adsorption.
Ge incorporation also changes the cluster geometry and electronic state.
The question addressed here is how these coupled changes affect binding at
individual Pt sites. In particular, the CO binding energy includes both the
cluster--CO interaction and the energy change associated with structural
reorganization. Comparing these contributions across sites and isomers can
help identify the origin of their different binding energies.

In this work, ten-atom \ce{Pt10} and
\ce{Pt_xGe_{10-x}} ($x=4$, 5, and 6) clusters are thoroughly examined using density functional theory and a variety of state-of-the-art chemical bonding methods to unravel the induced effect of Ge in the Pt--CO bond. Pt/Ge substitutions generate
derivative structures in order to isolate the effect of the geometry in the CO adsorption.
These derivatives isomers 
provide models for examining how local Pt environment and cluster
relaxation affect the CO binding. Combining local coordination and Pt--C bonding
descriptors with a fragment-energy decomposition in the  carbonyl
complexes, allows to disentangle the contributions of local bonding and structural reorganization to the overall CO binding energy.
This comparison goes beyond the overall effect of Ge content by examining
how local bonding and cluster reorganization contribute to differences in
CO binding between sites and isomers.
