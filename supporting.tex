\subsection*{Effective coordination number}
\label{sec:supporting_econ_definition}

The local environment of each Pt adsorption site was characterized with
Hoppe's effective coordination number (ECoN),\cite{hoppe:econ:1979} which
uses smooth distance-dependent weights instead of a fixed distance cutoff.
For a center atom \(i\), each neighbor \(j\) is first described by the scaled
distance
\begin{equation}
s_{ij}=\frac{d_{ij}}{r_i^{\rm cov}+r_j^{\rm cov}},
\end{equation}
where \(d_{ij}\) is the distance between the two atoms and \(r^{\rm cov}\) are
covalent radii reported by Cordero et al.\cite{cordero:covalent-radii:2008}
For the metal atoms considered here, we used
\(r_{\ce{Pt}}=1.36~\si{\angstromunit}\) and
\(r_{\ce{Ge}}=1.20~\si{\angstromunit}\). Each neighbor then contributes with
the weight
\begin{equation}
w_{ij}=\exp\left[1-\left(\frac{s_{ij}}{\bar{s}_i}\right)^6\right],
\end{equation}
and the effective coordination number is
\begin{equation}
ECoN_i=\sum_j w_{ij}.
\end{equation}
The reference distance \(\bar{s}_i\) is the weighted average of the same
scaled distances,
\begin{equation}
\bar{s}_i=\frac{\sum_j s_{ij}w_{ij}}{\sum_j w_{ij}},
\end{equation}
and is obtained iteratively until \(\bar{s}_i\) and the weights \(w_{ij}\)
are mutually consistent. Close neighbors therefore contribute almost as full
contacts, whereas distant atoms contribute only partially or negligibly.

ECoN descriptors were evaluated for each Pt site in the bare cluster before
\ce{CO} adsorption and, when indicated, for the optimized carbonyl. Only
metal neighbors were included. The same weights were partitioned according to
the element of each neighbor, giving
\(ECoN_{\rm total}^{0}=ECoN_{\rm Pt}^{0}+ECoN_{\rm Ge}^{0}\). The initial Ge
fraction is \(x_{\rm Ge}^{0}=ECoN_{\rm Ge}^{0}/ECoN_{\rm total}^{0}\), where
the superscript 0 denotes the bare-cluster geometry. A superscript \(\ce{CO}\)
denotes a descriptor evaluated for the optimized carbonyl after adsorption and
relaxation.

\subsubsection*{C--O bond length and stretching frequency}

The \ce{C-O} bond length and stretching frequency provide complementary
information about CO activation. The calculated harmonic frequency of free
\ce{CO} is 2129~\si{\per\centi\metre}; upon adsorption, the stretching
frequency decreases in all the carbonyls. Figure~\ref{fig:neutral_co_frequency}
compares the optimized bond lengths and stretching frequencies for the
derivative and GM carbonyls.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\input{neutral_co_frequency_figure}
\caption{Harmonic \ce{C-O} stretching frequency versus the optimized
\ce{C-O} distance for the carbonyls. Black, blue, vermilion, and green identify
\ce{Pt10CO}, \ce{Pt6Ge4CO}, \ce{Pt5Ge5CO}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6CO}, respectively.
Filled symbols denote derivative structures and open symbols denote GM
structures. Circles, squares, triangles, pentagons, and diamonds represent
singlet, triplet, quintet, septet, and nonet states, respectively. The dashed
black line is a visual guide through the filled \ce{Pt10CO} triplet and
quintet points. The dotted gray line is a visual guide through the region
occupied by most filled Pt--Ge singlet points; it is not a regression line.
The two filled \ce{Pt6Ge4CO} structures initiated at site class 1/4 and site 2
fall clearly outside this region.}
\label{fig:neutral_co_frequency}
\end{figure}

Badger's rule describes the relation between the equilibrium bond length and
the stretching force constant for a given pair of atoms.\cite{badger:1934,dabo:2012}
The filled derivative \ce{Pt10CO} points show the expected inverse relation:
longer \ce{C-O} bonds have lower stretching frequencies. The dashed black
line is a visual guide to this trend. Many filled Pt--Ge singlet points occupy
a different region, marked by the dotted gray line. Two derivative
\ce{Pt6Ge4CO} structures initiated at site class 1/4 and site 2 lie outside
this region. Both have among the longest \ce{C-O} bonds in this subset (about
1.16~\si{\angstromunit}) and stretching frequencies of 2018 and
2019~\si{\per\centi\metre}, respectively, about 110~\si{\per\centi\metre}
below the calculated harmonic frequency of free \ce{CO} at the same level of
theory (2129~\si{\per\centi\metre}). At both sites, the Pt atom bound to
\ce{CO} has two close Pt neighbors and one close Ge. These two sites have a
Pt-rich local environment, stronger adsorption, and longer \ce{C-O} bonds
than site 5/7, while their stretching frequencies are almost the same as at
site 5/7 (2019~\si{\per\centi\metre}). The GM points come from different
starting structures and spin states and are found both within and outside
these two regions. Within the derivative series, the average \ce{C-O}
distance is slightly longer for \ce{Pt10^{der}CO} than for the Ge-containing
carbonyls. This small difference suggests slightly greater \ce{C-O}
activation in the pure-Pt structures. The site values vary, and the comparison
includes different spin states, so this trend should be interpreted with care.
It is consistent with earlier DFT NBO\cite{ugartemendia:2021} and
PDOS\cite{ugartemendia:2022} analyses that associate Ge substitution with
reduced metal-to-\ce{CO} back-donation.
