Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, ``CO Adsorption on Pt and Pt--Ge Clusters: Effects of Composition, Starting Structure, and Cluster Reorganization'' for publication as an Original Research Article in the \textit{Journal of Cluster Science}.

Carbon monoxide poisoning is a major deactivation mechanism for Pt in various
catalytic applications, leading to large economic expenses associated with the
renewal of catalysts in industry. Previous studies have shown that Ge doping can
increase the resistance to CO poisoning. However, Ge incorporation into Pt also
changes the geometry and bonding pattern of Pt clusters, and the reasons for
weaker CO binding upon Ge incorporation remain unclear. In this work, using
model ten-atom subnanoclusters, we examine how composition, starting geometry,
and spin state affect CO binding and local bonding. The work is based on a
crossed-parent framework that uses the reported global-minimum structures of \ce{Pt10} and \ce{Pt5Ge5} as parent geometries for the substitution models. The main novelty of our work is the adoption of this derivative-structure approach, which provides a controlled way to examine the effects of composition, starting structure, and spin multiplicity on CO binding. The derivatives are subsequently complexed with CO, and {we analyze their electronic structure and bonding, together with local descriptors and a fragment-energy decomposition that separates frozen-fragment interaction from cluster reorganization}.

We argue that the binding energy to CO, {as a global descriptor, depends on both the cluster--CO complex and the chosen bare-cluster reference, and therefore does not by itself directly measure local Pt--CO bonding}. We highlight the need for caution when relying solely on the BE and {use complementary local descriptors of the carbonyl complex, including} the Pt--C distance, C--O stretching frequency, or electronic-structure analysis of the complex.
