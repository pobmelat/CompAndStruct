Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, ``How Composition and Cluster Structure Influence CO Adsorption in \ce{Pt} and Pt--Ge Clusters'' for publication as an Original Research Article in the \textit{Journal of Cluster Science}.

Carbon monoxide poisoning is a major deactivation mechanism for Pt in various catalytic applications leading to large economic expenses associated with the renewal of the catalysts in industry. Previous works showed that Ge doping can increase the resistance to CO poisoning. However, Ge incorporation into Pt also changes the geometry and bonding pattern of Pt clusters and it is unclear how Ge weakens the Pt--CO interaction. In this work, using model 10-atom subnano clusters, we determine the interplay between chemical bonding, geometry, spin state and tendency to undergo CO poisoning. The work is based on a crossed-parent framework, in which the geometry of ideal pure Pt and equimolar PtGe clusters is preserved. The derivatives are subsequently complexed to CO and their electronic structure, chemical bonding and affinities to CO are studied.

We argue that the binding energy to CO, being a global descriptor that depends not only on the energy of the cluster--CO complex but also on the energy of the reference states chosen for the bare cluster, is not necessarily the best descriptor for measuring CO affinity. We highlight the need for caution when relying solely on the BE and propose to additionally employ local descriptors that depend exclusively on the state of the carbonyl complex, rather than the bare cluster alone, such as the Pt--C distance, C--O stretching frequency, or electronic structure analysis of the complex.


The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration by another journal.
