Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, ``How Composition and Cluster Structure Influence CO Adsorption in \ce{Pt} and Pt--Ge Clusters'' for publication as an Original Research Article in the \textit{Journal of Cluster Science}.

Earlier studies showed that adding Ge to Pt clusters can weaken CO binding. However, the lowest-energy clusters also change shape as their composition changes. To examine the role of structure, we \textcolor{cyan}{adopted a derivative structure approach. By starting} started from the reported global minima of \ce{Pt10} and \ce{Pt5Ge5}, \textcolor{cyan}{we} exchanged selected Pt atoms for Ge or all Ge atoms for Pt, and optimized the resulting clusters. This gives us two \ce{Pt10} isomers that we can compare at the same spin multiplicity. Even with composition and spin held fixed, their CO binding energies differ by about 0.30~eV on average across the Pt sites.

In the \ce{Pt5Ge5} derivative, the frozen-fragment interaction favors one Pt site, yet CO binds 0.42~eV more strongly at the other. The energy decomposition shows that metal-cluster reorganization reverses the site preference.

\textcolor{cyan}{The combination of chemical bonding descriptors and fragment energy analysis employed in this work to deconvolute structural contributions to binding energies might provide comprehensive insight into helping rationalize the catalytic behaviour of clusters. We believe it fits particularly well within the scope of \textit{Journal of Cluster Science}, and will be of interest to researchers working on cluster-based computational catalysis and chemical bonding analysis.} 

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration by another journal.
