\subsection{Local environment and structural response}

We next examine local Pt coordination before and after
\ce{CO} adsorption, followed by the optimized \ce{Pt-C} distances and
electron densities at the Pt--C bond critical points.

\subsubsection{Local Pt coordination before and after CO adsorption}
\label{subsec:local_pt_coordination}

We use Hoppe's effective coordination number (ECoN) to describe the
local environment of each Pt adsorption site, separating the contributions
from neighboring Pt and Ge atoms. The descriptors are first evaluated for the
bare clusters and then for the optimized carbonyls, allowing us to compare the
initial site environment with its change after CO adsorption. The use of ECoN
is described in Methods; its definition and notation are given in the
Supporting Information, Section ``Effective coordination number.''

For the pure-Pt clusters, $ECoN_{\rm total}^{0}=ECoN_{\rm Pt}^{0}$.
The initial ECoN values for \ce{Pt10^{GM}} and \ce{Pt10^{der}} are given in
Supporting Table~\ref{tab:tfg_pt10_bare_econ}.
The two groups of Pt sites in the nonet \ce{Pt10^{GM}} have different initial
coordination but give almost identical binding energies. The six more highly
coordinated sites, with $ECoN^{0}$ close to 5.68, give
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})=-1.60$ eV, while the four sites with $ECoN^{0}$ close
to 3.00 give $-1.58$ eV. Thus, the
large difference in initial coordination is accompanied by only a small
difference in CO binding within this nonet series.
%The binding energies are given in Supporting Table~\ref{tab:tfg_adsorption_descriptors}.}

Hossain and co-workers found that \ce{CO} preferentially adsorbs at
less coordinated sites on Pt nanoparticles.\cite{hossain:lowcoord:2020}
The triplet \ce{Pt10^{der}} shows this tendency.
The most highly coordinated initial sites,
1 and 9, converge to the weakest-binding minimum ($-2.16$ eV), whereas site
6, with lower initial coordination, gives the strongest binding ($-2.66$ eV).
Sites 8 and 10 also illustrate this effect: although their initial
$ECoN^{0}$ values are 3.03 and 4.54, respectively, both relax to the same
carbonyl minimum, with a final $ECoN^{\ce{CO}}$ of 4.97 and a binding energy
of $-2.65$ eV
(Supporting Table~\ref{tab:tfg_pt10_der_econ_co}).

For Pt--Ge clusters, we also consider whether the
neighboring metal atoms are Pt or Ge.
For \ce{Pt6Ge4^{der}} (Table~\ref{tab:tfg_crossed_ptge_econ_descriptors}),
site 5/7 has the largest initial Ge contribution to
$ECoN_{\rm total}^{0}$ ($x_{\rm Ge}^{0}=$0.81, the fraction of the initial total ECoN
due to Ge) and gives the weakest CO binding in the series
($BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})=-1.57$ eV). By contrast, sites 2 and 1/4 combine substantial effective
Pt coordination with smaller $x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$ values and give three of the
strongest binding energies. This comparison is consistent with weaker CO
binding as the Ge contribution to the initial Pt environment increases.
{Site 9 is an exception. Although its initial Ge fraction is relatively
large ($x_{\rm Ge}^{0}=0.75$), its binding energy ($-2.25$ eV) is nearly the same as}
those of sites 2 and 1/4. Upon adsorption, however, the local environment of
site 9 changes appreciably: $ECoN_{\rm Pt}^{0}$ is 0.73 initially
and $ECoN_{\rm Pt}^{\ce{CO}}=1.16$ in the optimized carbonyl, while
$x_{\rm Ge}$ changes from 0.75 to 0.61. The corresponding changes at site
5/7 are smaller: $ECoN_{\rm Pt}^{0}$ is 0.53 initially and
$ECoN_{\rm Pt}^{\ce{CO}}=0.66$ in the optimized carbonyl, while
$x_{\rm Ge}$ changes from 0.81 to 0.77. Thus, after adsorption, the site-9 carbonyl
has a larger effective Pt contribution and a smaller Ge fraction than its
initial environment. The initial local composition therefore does not by
itself determine the binding energy.
The descriptors of the optimized carbonyls are reported in Supporting
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_crossed_ptge_econ_co_descriptors}.

The $ECoN_{\rm Pt}^{0}$ and $ECoN_{\rm Ge}^{0}$ components, which measure the
effective contributions from neighboring Pt and Ge atoms, respectively,
provide complementary information for \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}}.
Site 4, which gives the weakest adsorption in this composition, has
$x_{\rm Ge}^{0}\simeq1.00$ and essentially no effective Pt coordination. By
contrast, sites 6/8 and 7/10 have almost identical $x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$ values,
but site 6/8 has a higher initial $ECoN_{\rm total}^{0}$ than site 7/10
(5.58 vs 2.91) and binds CO more strongly by 0.42~eV.
After CO adsorption, the two sites still differ markedly in
$ECoN_{\rm total}^{\ce{CO}}$ (5.69 for site 6/8 and 2.95 for site 7/10;
Supporting Table~\ref{tab:tfg_crossed_ptge_econ_co_descriptors}). In both
carbonyls,
$x_{\rm Ge}$ changes from 0.75 initially to
$x_{\rm Ge}^{\ce{CO}}=0.70$ for site 6/8, and from 0.75 to
$x_{\rm Ge}^{\ce{CO}}=0.68$ for site 7/10,
indicating a shift in the local coordination balance toward Pt.
The fragment analysis in
Section~\ref{subsec:interaction_fragment_reorganization} examines the energy
contributions to this 0.42~eV binding-energy difference.

For \ce{Pt4Ge6^{der}}, all sampled sites are Ge-rich, but the pre-adsorption
descriptors clarify the weakest-binding structure. Site 1 has the largest
initial Ge fraction ($x_{\rm Ge}^{0}=0.98$) and gives the weakest CO binding
in this set 
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})=-1.05$ eV), compared with
$-1.31$ eV for site 2 and
$-1.46$ eV for sites 5 and 7. These three sites
have larger initial effective Pt contributions than site 1. Upon adsorption,
however, site 1 undergoes substantial local reorganization:
$ECoN_{\rm Pt}^{\ce{CO}}$ increases to 1.42 and
$x_{\rm Ge}^{\ce{CO}}$ falls to 0.76, indicating that its local metal
environment becomes less Ge-dominated after relaxation. The ECoN descriptors of the optimized
derivative carbonyls are reported in Supporting
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_crossed_ptge_econ_co_descriptors}, while the corresponding
final-state descriptors for the GM structures are given in Supporting
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_gm_ptge_econ_descriptors}.

We next use the GM series to examine whether the relation between the
initial Pt/Ge environment and CO binding found above persists when the starting
cluster isomer is changed. The initial descriptors for these GM structures
are given in Supporting Table~\ref{tab:tfg_gm_ptge_econ_pre_descriptors}.
For \ce{Pt6Ge4^{GM}}, the four sites with
$x_{\rm Ge}^{0}=0.34$--0.56 give the strongest binding energies
($-1.95$ to $-1.66$~eV), whereas sites 4 and 5 have more Ge-rich initial
environments ($x_{\rm Ge}^{0}=0.81$ and 0.80) and give the weakest values
($-1.21$ and $-1.22$~eV). A similar distinction is found for \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}}:
sites 1, 2, 4, and 5
have $x_{\rm Ge}^{0}\simeq0.93$ and converge to the same carbonyl minimum,
whereas site 3 is almost completely coordinated by Ge
($x_{\rm Ge}^{0}\simeq1.00$) and gives the weakest binding. The \ce{Pt4Ge6^{GM}}
series is less regular. All four sites are Ge-rich. Sites 2 and 3 have similar
initial Ge fractions (0.92 and 0.91), yet they give the strongest and weakest
binding energies, respectively ($-1.51$ and $-1.05$ eV).
Overall, the initial ECoN descriptors suggest that Pt sites with a
larger Ge contribution tend to bind \ce{CO} less strongly. The site-9
exception in \ce{Pt6Ge4^{der}} and the variation across the
\ce{Pt4Ge6^{GM}} series show that the local coordination provides useful
context for the binding energies.
We next examine the optimized \ce{Pt-C} distances and
$\rho_{\rm BCP}$ values to describe the local bonds in the carbonyls.

\subsubsection{Pt--C distance and CO binding energy}

\begin{figure}[H]
	\begin{center}

	\end{center}
\caption{State-specific adiabatic \ce{CO} binding energy versus the optimized

\ce{Pt6Ge4CO}, \ce{Pt5Ge5CO}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6CO}, respectively. Filled symbols

\label{fig:neutral_be_vs_ptc}

Figure~\ref{fig:neutral_be_vs_ptc} shows an overall tendency: carbonyls
with shorter optimized \ce{Pt-C} distances generally have more negative
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ values. The plot also includes sites with similar
\ce{Pt-C} distances but very different binding energies.

For the \ce{Pt10^{der}CO}
series, the triplet minima have very similar \ce{Pt-C} distances,
1.83--1.84~\si{\angstromunit}, although their binding energies span about 0.50 eV (black
filled squares in
Figure~\ref{fig:neutral_be_vs_ptc}). The
quintet minima (black filled triangles) show the same pattern. Their distances
cover only 1.83--1.84~\si{\angstromunit}, whereas their binding energies span
about 0.64 eV. The GM points (open symbols) cover a wider range of
\ce{Pt-C} distances across different starting structures and spin states. The
binding-energy differences across the GM points therefore reflect variation in
\ce{Pt-C} distance, starting structure, and spin state.

{The Pt--Ge carbonyls extend the sampled \ce{Pt-C} distance range to
about 1.96~\si{\angstromunit}. A clear example is provided by sites 6/8 and
7/10 of \ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}CO}: their optimized \ce{Pt-C} distances are similar
(1.90 and 1.89~\si{\angstromunit}), but their binding energies differ by
about 0.42 eV (-2.08 and -1.66 eV, respectively). The fragment-energy
analysis below examines the contributions to this difference.}

The AIM analysis provides a complementary local descriptor of the
optimized \ce{Pt-C} bonds: the electron density at the bond critical point,
$\rho_{\rm BCP}$ (Table~\ref{tab:tfg_interaction_deformation}). For the
selected strongest- and weakest-binding pairs of \ce{Pt10}, \ce{Pt6Ge4}, and
\ce{Pt5Ge5} (sites 6 versus 1/9, 2 versus 5/7, and 6/8 versus 4,
respectively), the more strongly bound carbonyl has the larger
$\rho_{\rm BCP}$. This ordering is not consistent across all comparisons:
sites 6/8 and 7/10 of \ce{Pt5Ge5} have nearly equal densities (0.173 and
0.175 $e\,a_0^{-3}$) despite a 0.42~eV difference in binding energy, while
site 1 of \ce{Pt4Ge6} has a larger density than site 5/7 (0.181 versus
0.171 $e\,a_0^{-3}$) but binds \ce{CO} less strongly. These examples show
that $\rho_{\rm BCP}$ describes the local \ce{Pt-C} bond, while the binding
energy also reflects the interaction and structural reorganization of the
cluster. The fragment-energy analysis in Section~\ref{subsec:interaction_fragment_reorganization}
examines these contributions.
