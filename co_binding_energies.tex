\subsection[CO binding energies]{\ce{CO} binding energies}

We next compare the \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) values for the \ce{Pt10^{der}CO} and
the \ce{Pt_xGe_{10-x}^{der}CO} series ($x=6,5,4$). We also examine the GM calculations to see
how the starting structure and multiplicity affect the binding energies. For
each model, \ce{CO} was placed at each independent Pt site. The labels in
Supporting Figure~\ref{fig:tfg_site_numbering} identify these initial
placements. Because the clusters are flexible, different initial placements
can relax to the same optimized carbonyl structure.

In the triplet \ce{Pt10^{der}CO} series, the minima reached
from sites 6 and 8/10 have very similar binding energies ($-2.66$ and
$-2.65$ eV, respectively), whereas calculations started at sites 1 and 9
converge to the weakest-binding minimum. Averaged over all ten initial Pt sites,
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ is $-2.50$ eV; the seven distinct optimized minima
span $-2.66$ to $-2.16$ eV. For the quintet \ce{Pt10^{der}CO} series, the
mean over the ten initial Pt sites is $-2.28$ eV, and the five distinct
optimized minima span $-2.65$ to $-2.01$ eV.
The strongest binding in the quintet series, reached from
sites 5/7, is therefore very similar to that in the triplet series. The
two multiplicities differ mainly in the distribution and mean of the
binding energies.

\red{In the \ce{Pt10^{GM}CO} series, the nonet carbonyls bind weakly and
span a narrow range ($-1.60$ to $-1.58$ eV). The septet and quintet
sets span from $-2.35$ to $-1.75$ eV and from $-2.28$ to $-1.55$ eV,
respectively.

The nonet values are less negative than the \ce{Pt10}--\ce{CO} binding energy
reported by Ugartemendia \textit{et al.}\cite{ugartemendia:2022}, and the two
results use different bare-cluster references. In the present series, all ten
values use the same optimized bare nonet \ce{Pt10} structure
($E_0=-1194.335917\ E_h$). In the earlier work, the bare-cluster energy was
calculated after removing \ce{CO} from the optimized complex and optimizing
the remaining cluster. The two binding energies therefore refer to different
bare-cluster structures, so their numerical difference cannot be assigned to a
change in the \ce{Pt}--\ce{CO} interaction alone.}

To examine the effect of the starting structure at fixed multiplicity,
we compared the quintet adsorption series of \ce{Pt10^{der}} and
\ce{Pt10^{GM}}. At the quintet multiplicity, the bare \ce{Pt10^{der}} cluster lies 1.17 eV above the bare \ce{Pt10^{GM}} cluster. For the corresponding CO adsorption series, the mean \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\), calculated over the initial Pt sites, is \(-2.28\) eV for \ce{Pt10^{der}} and \(-1.98\) eV for \ce{Pt10^{GM}}. Thus, the mean is 0.30 eV more negative for \ce{Pt10^{der}}. The two isomers therefore give different mean CO binding energies even at the same composition and multiplicity.

Across the Pt--Ge derivative series, the mean
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ becomes less negative as the Ge
content increases, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:tfg_adsorption_distribution}.
The binding energies vary between Pt sites, especially for
\ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}CO}, whose values span $0.73$ eV.
The weaker mean \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) with increasing Ge content is consistent with earlier reports of weaker CO binding on Pt–Ge clusters.
\cite{ugartemendia:2021,ugartemendia:2022}


The GM results are shown as dashed blue data in
Figure~\ref{fig:tfg_adsorption_distribution}. Each carbonyl has the same
multiplicity as its bare-cluster GM: triplet for \ce{Pt6Ge4^{GM}} and
singlet for \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}} and \ce{Pt4Ge6^{GM}}.
Compared with the derivatives, \ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}CO} binds \ce{CO} less
strongly, while \ce{Pt4Ge6^{GM}CO} gives a similar range of binding
energies. The \ce{Pt6Ge4^{GM}CO} series also shows weaker binding than
\ce{Pt6Ge4^{der}CO}. In this case, both the starting structure and
multiplicity change, since the GM series is triplet and the derivative
series is singlet.
