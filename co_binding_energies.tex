\subsection[CO binding energies]{\ce{CO} binding energies}

We next compare adsorbing CO in all the possible binding sites of the clusters that are not symmetrically equivalent. It is important to note that CO does not adsorb on Ge, but exclusively adsorbs on Pt.  For \ce{Pt10^{der}CO}, placing \ce{CO} initially at the ten Pt sites gives seven distinct optimized minima, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:tfg_pt10co_series}. The resulting optimized carbonyl structures of PtGe are shown in  Figures~\ref{fig:tfg_ptgeco_series}.
The \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) values for \ce{Pt10^{der}CO} and the
\ce{Pt_xGe_{10-x}^{der}CO} complexes ($x=6,5,4$) are reported in Supporting
Tables~\ref{tab:tfg_adsorption_descriptors} and
\ref{tab:tfg_bimetallic_adsorption_descriptors}. The corresponding data for the
GM structures are given in Supporting Table~\ref{tab:tfg_gm_bimetallic_adsorption_descriptors}.
Additional ECoN descriptors are reported in Supporting Tables~\ref{tab:tfg_pt10_der_econ_co}
and~\ref{tab:tfg_gm_ptge_econ_descriptors}. We also examine the GM clusters to see
how the starting geometry and multiplicity affect the binding energies.

In the triplet \ce{Pt10^{der}CO} complexes, the most stable adsorption site is found at sites 6 and 8/10 ( binding energies $-2.66$ and
$-2.65$ eV, respectively), whereas calculations started at sites 1 and 9
converge to the weakest-binding minimum $-2.16$ eV. Averaged over all ten Pt sites,
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ is $-2.50$ eV; the seven distinct optimized minima
span from $-2.66$ to $-2.16$ eV. As mentioned before, \ce{Pt10^{der}}  in the triplet and quintet spin-states are nearly degenerate. For this reason, the quintet \ce{Pt10^{der}CO} complexes were also investigated, and the
mean over the ten initial Pt sites is $-2.28$ eV, and the five distinct
optimized minima span from $-2.65$ to $-2.01$ eV.
The strongest binding among the \ce{Pt10^{der}CO} complexes in the quintet spin-state is found when CO is adsorbed on 
sites 5/7, and it is therefore very similar to that in the triplet series. The
two multiplicities differ mainly in the distribution and mean of the
binding energies although the overall values are similar.

When CO adsorbs on \ce{Pt10^{GM}}(nonet) it binds weakly and the binding sites
are more homogeneous due to the symmetry of the structure ($BE_{\rm
ad}(\ce{CO})$: $-1.60$ to $-1.58$ eV). \ce{Pt10^{GM}} in a septet and quintet
spin states bind more strongly CO with $BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ ranging from
$-2.35$ to $-1.75$ eV and from $-2.28$ to $-1.55$ eV, respectively. Note that
the previously reported \ce{Pt10}--\ce{CO} binding energy is defined differently
since the two results use different bare-cluster references
\cite{ugartemendia:2022}. In the present work, the same optimized bare nonet
\ce{Pt10} structure is used as reference in the previous work the bare-cluster energy was calculated after removing \ce{CO} from the complex and optimizing the remaining cluster.


To isolate the effect of the starting geometry, we compared the CO binding
energies of  \ce{Pt10^{der}} and \ce{Pt10^{GM}} in their quintet spin state,
which is the most favored common spin state for both isomers.  In the quintet
multiplicity,  \ce{Pt10^{der}} is 1.17 eV less stable than the  \ce{Pt10^{GM}}
isomer. Upon CO adsorption, the mean \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) is \(-2.28\) eV
for \ce{Pt10^{der}} and \(-1.98\) eV for \ce{Pt10^{GM}}. Therefore, when the
composition and multiplicity are fixed, the binding energy \sout{interaction
	strength}\red{mejor \textbf{binding energy} que lo hemos definido como el BE adiabatico,
intearation strength puede ser otra forma de BE} between Pt subnanoclusters and CO changes with the geometry. 

Across the Pt--Ge derivative series, the mean
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ becomes less negative as the Ge
content increases, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:tfg_adsorption_distribution}.
\ce{Pt5Ge5^{der}CO} shows the largest variation in the \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\)
values, which span $0.73$ eV,\sout{and the weakest interaction with CO,}\red{No
es correcto} in agreement
with previous works \cite{ugartemendia:2022,poths:2023}.
The weaker mean \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) with increasing Ge content is consistent with earlier reports of weaker CO binding on \ce{Pt-Ge} clusters, even if the geometries are not the most stable.
\cite{ugartemendia:2021,ugartemendia:2022}

\ce{Pt5Ge5^{GM}CO} and \ce{Pt6Ge4^{GM}CO}.
