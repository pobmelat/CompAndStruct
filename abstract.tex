\abstract{
Ge incorporation can weaken CO adsorption on Pt clusters, but it also changes
their geometry and bonding. We combine density functional theory with advanced chemical bonding
analysis to examine the interplay between composition, geometry, electronic structure, and CO adsorption
 in ten-atom \ce{Pt10}, \ce{Pt6Ge4}, \ce{Pt5Ge5}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6}
clusters. By generating derivative structures through Pt/Ge substitution in previously reported global-minimum geometries, we systematically examine the influence of composition, structural arrangement, and spin state. Chemical bonding analysis reveals a transition from predominantly delocalized multicenter bonding in pure Pt clusters to more localized covalent bonding in Pt–Ge clusters. Effective coordination numbers, Pt–C bond lengths, and bond critical point electron densities are then used to characterize the local environment and bonding of adsorbed CO. However, these descriptors do not consistently correlate with CO binding energies. Energy decomposition reveals that adsorption strength reflects a composition- and site-dependent balance between the frozen-fragment Pt–CO interaction and metal-cluster reorganization. Remarkably, structural reorganization can even reverse the binding-energy trend predicted from the interaction energy alone. These findings demonstrate that CO binding energy, which depends on both the adsorbed complex and the chosen bare-cluster reference, does not necessarily reflect the intrinsic strength of the Pt–CO interaction. We therefore highlight the importance of combining adsorption energies with local bonding descriptors and fragment-energy analysis to disentangle electronic and structural contributions to CO affinity in bimetallic nanoclusters.
