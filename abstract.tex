\abstract{
Ge incorporation can weaken CO adsorption on Pt clusters, but it also changes
their geometry and bonding. We use density functional theory to examine these
coupled effects in ten-atom \ce{Pt10}, \ce{Pt6Ge4}, \ce{Pt5Ge5}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6}
clusters. Pt/Ge substitutions starting from previously reported global-minimum
structures generate derivative models for comparing composition and starting
geometry. CO adsorption is examined at every independent Pt site, with
additional calculations on reoptimized reported global-minimum structures.
Within the Pt--Ge derivative series, the mean state-specific adiabatic CO
binding energy becomes less negative as the Ge content increases. Adaptive
natural density partitioning describes delocalized multicenter bonding in the
\ce{Pt10} derivative and localized one- and two-center bonding in the Pt--Ge
derivatives. Comparing two \ce{Pt10} isomers at the same quintet multiplicity
gives a difference of 0.30 eV in mean CO binding energy, showing the effect of
structure at fixed composition and multiplicity. Effective coordination
numbers characterize the local Pt/Ge environments, while optimized Pt--C
distances and bond critical point densities describe the local bond. The
fragment-energy decomposition shows that binding-energy differences arise
from different balances between frozen-fragment interaction and metal-cluster
reorganization. In the selected \ce{Pt5Ge5} comparisons, reorganization can
favor stronger binding even when the frozen-fragment interaction favors the
other site. Combining binding energies with local descriptors and fragment
energies therefore helps distinguish local Pt--CO bonding from the structural
response of the cluster to adsorption.
}
