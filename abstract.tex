\abstract{
Carbon monoxide poisoning is a major deactivation mechanism for Pt in various catalytic applications. Ge incorporation can weaken CO adsorption on Pt clusters, but it also changes
their geometry and bonding. Here we combine density functional theory with advanced chemical bonding
analysis to examine the interplay between composition, geometry, electronic structure, and CO adsorption
 in ten-atom \ce{Pt10}, \ce{Pt6Ge4}, \ce{Pt5Ge5}, and \ce{Pt4Ge6}
model clusters. By generating derivative structures through Pt/Ge substitution in
previously reported global-minimum geometries, we examine how composition and
starting structure affect CO binding. For the two pure Pt isomers, we also
compare CO binding at the same spin multiplicity. Chemical bonding analysis
reveals a transition from predominantly delocalized multicenter bonding in pure
Pt clusters to more localized covalent bonding in Pt–Ge clusters. Effective
coordination numbers, Pt–C bond lengths, and bond critical point electron
densities are then used to characterize the local environment and bonding of
adsorbed CO. However, these descriptors do not consistently correlate with CO
binding energies. Energy decomposition reveals that adsorption strength reflects
a composition- and site-dependent balance between the frozen-fragment
cluster--CO interaction and metal-cluster reorganization. Remarkably, structural
reorganization can even reverse the binding-energy trend predicted from the
interaction energy alone. These findings demonstrate that CO binding energy,
which depends on both the adsorbed complex and the chosen bare-cluster
reference, does not necessarily reflect the intrinsic strength of the Pt–CO
interaction. We therefore highlight the importance of combining adsorption
energies with local bonding descriptors and fragment-energy analysis to
disentangle electronic and structural contributions to CO affinity in carbonyl complexes.}
