\subsection{Interaction and fragment reorganization}
\label{subsec:interaction_fragment_reorganization}
To complement the ECoN analysis, we separated the electronic
binding energy of selected carbonyls into the frozen-fragment interaction and
the energy changes from metal-cluster and \ce{CO} reorganization.
Related calculations with frozen and relaxed Pt clusters have been
used to examine how structural relaxation contributes to \ce{CO}
adsorption energies.\cite{gasper:2017} They found that the
\ce{CO}--Pt interaction was the main contribution to adsorption,
with a smaller contribution from cluster relaxation.

\subsubsection{Energy decomposition and \ce{Pt10} comparison}

We compared the fragment-energy terms for three triplet
\ce{Pt10^{der}CO} minima: the strongest-binding minimum reached from site 6,
the weakest-binding minimum reached from sites 1/9, and the minimum reached
from sites 8/10. The minima reached from site 6 and sites 8/10 have similar
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ values and broadly similar energy-decomposition terms;
all values are given in Table~\ref{tab:tfg_interaction_deformation}. All three
started from the same \ce{Pt10^{der}} structure.
For each
optimized carbonyl,
we calculated the energies of the isolated \ce{Pt10} and \ce{CO}
fragments at the geometries they have in the optimized complex, without
relaxing either fragment. These frozen geometries are denoted by
\(\mathbf R_{\ce{Pt10}}^{\ce{Pt10CO}}\) and
\(\mathbf R_{\ce{CO}}^{\ce{Pt10CO}}\), respectively. The interaction energy is the energy
of the optimized complex minus the energies of these two frozen fragments:
\begin{align}
E_{\mathrm{int}} &=
E_{\ce{Pt10CO}}(\mathbf R_{\ce{Pt10CO}})
-E_{\ce{Pt10}}(\mathbf R_{\ce{Pt10}}^{\ce{Pt10CO}})
-E_{\ce{CO}}(\mathbf R_{\ce{CO}}^{\ce{Pt10CO}}),\\
E_{\mathrm{def}} &=
\left[
E_{\ce{Pt10}}(\mathbf R_{\ce{Pt10}}^{\ce{Pt10CO}})
-E_{\ce{Pt10}}(\mathbf R_{\ce{Pt10}}^{0})
\right]
{}+
\left[
E_{\ce{CO}}(\mathbf R_{\ce{CO}}^{\ce{Pt10CO}})
-E_{\ce{CO}}(\mathbf R_{\ce{CO}}^{0})
\right],
\label{eq:tfg_deformation}
\end{align}

For the \ce{Pt10^{der}CO} minima,
\(\mathbf R_{\ce{Pt10}}^0\) is the
\ce{Pt10^{der}} reference, and
\(\mathbf R_{\ce{CO}}^0\) is the optimized isolated singlet \ce{CO}. Thus, the
same bare-cluster reference is used for the three carbonyl structures.
$E_{\mathrm{int}}$ describes the electronic stabilization obtained when
the two frozen fragments are brought together at the geometry of the complex.
The deformation energy, $E_{\mathrm{def}}$, is the sum of the
metal-cluster and \ce{CO} terms in Eq.~\ref{eq:tfg_deformation}. It measures
the energy change as each fragment moves from its optimized isolated
geometry to the geometry it has in the carbonyl. Positive values indicate
destabilization
and negative values indicate stabilization.
The two bracketed terms are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_interaction_deformation} as
$E_{\rm reorg}^{\rm metal}$ and $E_{\rm reorg}^{\ce{CO}}$, respectively;
therefore,
$\Delta E_{\mathrm{elec}}=E_{\mathrm{int}}+E_{\mathrm{def}}$, where
$\Delta E_{\mathrm{elec}}$ is the electronic contribution to the binding
energy before the zero-point vibrational correction. The zero-point correction
is then added to obtain the final $BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$.


For the site 6 carbonyl, \(E_{\rm int}=-2.71\) eV. The
metal-cluster and \ce{CO} contributions to \(E_{\rm def}\) are \(-0.06\) eV
and \(0.03\) eV, respectively. The negative metal term indicates that the
\ce{Pt10} fragment is stabilized at the geometry it adopts in the carbonyl,
whereas the positive \ce{CO} term indicates a small destabilization at its
carbonyl geometry. Together, these terms give \(E_{\rm def}=-0.03\) eV and
\(\Delta E_{\rm elec}=-2.74\) eV.

For the weaker minimum reached from sites 1/9, \(E_{\rm int}=-2.46\) eV. The
metal-cluster and \ce{CO} contributions are \(0.20\) and \(0.02\) eV,
respectively, giving \(E_{\rm def}=0.22\) eV and
\(\Delta E_{\rm elec}=-2.24\) eV. 
The site 6 Pt--CO interaction is therefore
0.25 eV more stabilizing, and its $E_{\rm reorg}^{\rm metal}$ 
term is also more favorable for site 6: \(-0.06\) eV, compared with \(0.20\) eV
for the site 1/9 minimum, a difference of \(0.26\) eV.
Together, these contributions give a 0.50 eV difference in
\(\Delta E_{\rm elec}\). Thus, the stronger \(\Delta E_{\rm elec}\) for site 6 reflects both a more stabilizing Pt–CO interaction and a more favorable metal-cluster reorganization.
The full decomposition and the corresponding
\(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) values are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:tfg_interaction_deformation}.


Across the selected minima in Table~\ref{tab:tfg_interaction_deformation},
the \ce{CO}-reorganization contribution is small and nearly constant
(0.02 to 0.03 eV). The comparisons below therefore focus on the
frozen-fragment interaction and metal reorganization.

\subsubsection{Interaction and reorganization in Pt--Ge clusters}

We then applied the same analysis to selected strong- and
weak-binding minima of the three Pt--Ge derivatives. For \ce{Pt5Ge5}, we also
examined sites 6/8 and 7/10 because their initial $x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$ values are
similar, but their total ECoN and binding energies differ.

For \ce{Pt6Ge4}, the 0.68 eV stronger \(BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})\) at site 2 is mainly associated
with its more stabilizing frozen-fragment interaction, which is 0.79 eV more
favorable than at site 5/7. Its metal-reorganization term is 0.08 eV less
favorable, which slightly reduces this advantage.
The lower \(x_{\rm Ge}^{0}\) at site 2 is consistent
with its stronger binding. However, site 2 also has a higher
\(ECoN_{\rm total}^{0}\) than site 5/7 and still binds \ce{CO} more strongly,
contrary to the expectation based on lower coordination.
The fragment terms show that the difference in binding energy is mainly associated with \(E_{\rm int}\).

For \(\ce{Pt4Ge6}\), sites 5/7 bind \(\ce{CO}\) 0.41 eV more strongly than site 1. The frozen-fragment interaction at sites 5/7 is 0.23 eV more stabilizing (\(-1.89\) versus \(-1.66\) eV), and their metal-reorganization term is 0.18 eV more favorable (0.34 versus 0.52 eV). Both contributions therefore
account for the stronger binding at sites 5/7. The initial ECoN descriptors
show that site 1 has a larger $x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$ (0.98 versus 0.86 and 0.92)
and a higher $ECoN_{\rm total}^{0}$ (4.41 versus 3.99 for site 5 and 3.48
for site 7).The weaker binding at site 1 is consistent with both its
larger initial Ge fraction and its higher initial total coordination compared with sites 5/7..

For \ce{Pt5Ge5}, site 6/8 binds \ce{CO} 0.74 eV more
strongly than site 4. The metal-reorganization term is $-0.40$ eV for
site 6/8 and 0.49 eV for site 4, favoring site 6/8 by 0.89 eV.
The frozen-fragment interaction favors site 4 by only 0.14 eV
($-1.92$ versus $-1.78$ eV). The larger difference in metal reorganization
therefore gives stronger \ce{CO} binding at site 6/8.
The lower initial $x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$ at site 6/8 (0.75 versus 1.00) is
consistent with its stronger binding, even though this site is initially
more highly coordinated ($ECoN_{\rm total}^{0}=5.58$ versus 2.95).

The ECoN analysis in Section~\ref{subsec:local_pt_coordination} showed that
sites 6/8 and 7/10 of \ce{Pt5Ge5} have almost identical
$x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$ values 0.75 at both sites), but different
$ECoN_{\rm total}^{0}$ values (5.58 and 2.91) and
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ values ($-2.08$ and $-1.66$ eV, respectively).
$ECoN_{\rm total}^{0}$ sums the effective contributions from Pt and Ge
neighbors. Its higher value for site 6/8 indicates a more highly coordinated
initial Pt environment, while the similar $x_{\rm Ge}^{0}$ values show that
the relative Ge contribution is nearly the same at both sites.
Lower initial coordination would suggest stronger binding
at site 7/10, whereas site 6/8 binds \ce{CO} more strongly by 0.42 eV.
To examine this, we compare the fragment-energy
contributions for the two minima, separating the interaction at the optimized
carbonyl geometries from the energy changes associated with fragment
reorganization. Site 7/10 has a
more stabilizing frozen-fragment interaction than site 6/8
($-1.92$ versus $-1.78$ eV), but its metal-reorganization term is positive
($0.17$ eV), whereas that of site 6/8 is stabilizing ($-0.40$ eV). This
difference in metal reorganization is larger than the interaction advantage
of site 7/10. As a result, the electronic binding energy is less negative
for site 7/10 ($-1.73$ versus $-2.15$ eV), consistent with the final
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ values ($-1.66$ versus $-2.08$ eV).

Although lower coordination often favors \ce{CO}
binding on Pt nanoparticles,\cite{hossain:lowcoord:2020} the Pt--Ge cluster
examples show that the local Ge fraction and metal-cluster reorganization
also affect the binding-energy order.
Taken together, these results show that the calculated
$BE_{\rm ad}(\ce{CO})$ depends on several effects. The frozen-fragment
interaction and metal-cluster reorganization contribute in different
proportions across sites and compositions. ECoN describes the initial Pt
environment, while the fragment analysis shows how interaction and structural
reorganization combine in the final binding energy.
